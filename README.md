## ❓ What are Design Patterns?

Design Patterns are reusable, time-tested solutions to recurring software engineering problems. They were popularized by Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson, and John Vlissides in their seminal 1994 book, _Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software_ (commonly referred to as the "Gang of Four" or **GoF**).

While originally demonstrated using C++ and Smalltalk, design patterns have transcended specific languages. Today, design patterns form the core architecture of popular frameworks, standard libraries, and everyday production code.

## 📚 Curriculum & Covered Patterns

This course covers the full suite of **SOLID design principles** alongside all **23 classic GoF design patterns**:

### 1. SOLID Design Principles

- **S**ingle Responsibility Principle (SRP)
- **O**pen-Closed Principle (OCP)
- **L**iskov Substitution Principle (LSP)
- **I**nterface Segregation Principle (ISP)
- **D**ependency Inversion Principle (DIP)

### 2. Creational Patterns

Focus on mechanisms for object creation, enhancing flexibility and reuse of existing code.

- Builder
- Factories (Factory Method & Abstract Factory)
- Prototype
- Singleton

### 3. Structural Patterns

Focus on how to assemble objects and classes into larger structures while keeping them flexible and efficient.

- Adapter
- Bridge
- Composite
- Decorator
- Façade
- Flyweight
- Proxy

### 4. Behavioral Patterns

Focus on effective communication and assignment of responsibilities between objects.

- Chain of Responsibility
- Command
- Interpreter
- Iterator
- Mediator
- Memento
- Observer
- State
- Strategy
- Template Method
- Visitor
